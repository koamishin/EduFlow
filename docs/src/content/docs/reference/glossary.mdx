## Everyday finance terms

**Collection.** Money the institution has already received. An expected fee or uploaded payment
request is not yet a collection.

**Collection batch.** A recorded receipt or aggregate report, with its amount, dates, source, and restrictions.

**Restricted money.** Money that may only be used for a stated purpose. Approval of its record does not
remove that restriction.

**Allocation.** An approved budget allowance. It says what may be spent, not how much cash has arrived.

**Available cash / cash headroom.** Money left for the selected bills after the recorded restrictions, reserve, outflows,
and other commitments are accounted for.

**Commitment.** Money already promised or reserved for another obligation. Do not count it again as free cash.

**Reserve.** An amount protected from ordinary spending under the institution's controls.

## Records and decisions

**Planning snapshot.** A saved picture of budget, cash, source evidence, and selected bills at a specific
time. Later changes need a new context.

**Closed bill set.** The specific list staff selected for one plan. The planner does not add other bills.

**Payment intent.** An exact draft of a proposed payment: what bill, amount, recipient, wallet, and network.

**Funding window.** A short-lived record tying payment preparation to reviewed funding evidence.

**Reservation / capacity hold.** An application record holding room for a bill and its maximum fee. It does not stop
someone using the provider wallet outside OpenSinop.

**Authorization.** Recorded permission for one exact payment under the reviewed path. It is not proof
that the provider accepted the request or that money arrived.

**Reconciliation.** Comparing records with evidence and bringing an already occurred movement into the
appropriate record. It must not count the same money twice.

## Payment technology

**USDC.** A digital asset issued by Circle. OpenSinop's USDC payment amounts use six decimal places.

**Arc Testnet.** The test network used by the current payment workflows. Its chain ID is `5042002`.
Test funds are not real school revenue or a real supplier payment.

**Provider handle.** A request ID returned by the payment provider. It is different from an onchain transaction hash.

**Transaction hash.** An identifier for an onchain transaction. A hash alone does not establish that the
expected payment succeeded.

**Settlement.** Successful matching movement checked against the expected payment. Testnet settlement
still proves only a testnet movement.

**Worker and scheduler.** A worker processes queued jobs. A scheduler periodically starts dispatch or recovery
commands. A healthy web page does not prove either is running.

**Digest.** A fingerprint of recorded content used to detect changes. It is not a person's
signature or independent proof that the original document is authentic.

**MFA / TOTP.** Multi-factor authentication. A TOTP is the short-lived code from an authenticator app.
Normal payment review requires the reviewer's own current credentials.

**JEV / policy advice.** Labels used for the application's policy classification and evaluation features.
A match or high confidence score is advice, not permission to spend.
